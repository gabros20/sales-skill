# Surface: Enterprise Team

Purpose: State how the sales motion changes for complex, multi-stakeholder enterprise deals — a
named stage-gate model with published exit criteria, formal qualification, a buying group large
enough to need internal deconfliction, and a handoff to `success` that formalizes at a dollar
threshold rather than happening informally.

Read when:
- The deal has multiple stakeholders, a long cycle, and a size that justifies formal qualification
  overhead.
- The question is which named stage/qualification exhibit to point to, or how to cite a real
  enterprise process without over-claiming what it proves.

Skip when:
- One person is closing their own deals — go to [surface-founder-led-solo](surface-founder-led-solo.md),
  the DEFAULT surface.
- The motion is high-volume and low-ACV — go to [surface-smb-transactional](surface-smb-transactional.md).

Inputs: a qualified opportunity and a named buying group.

Produces: a stage-gate reference model with exit criteria, qualification-framework citation
discipline, and the enterprise instance of the sales↔success handoff.

## 1. The stage-gate exhibit: GitLab's published opportunity stages

GitLab publishes the fullest real stage-gate artifact found anywhere in this pack's research: ten
named opportunity stages, each with primary/secondary activities and an explicit exit-criteria
list (`content/handbook/sales/commercial/comm-sales-opp-stages/_index.md`,
https://gitlab.com/gitlab-com/content-sites/handbook/-/blob/46180fa3686c8baa55f91bf32c6e1d676eefd475/content/handbook/sales/commercial/comm-sales-opp-stages/_index.md,
GitLab handbook, CC BY-SA 4.0 — quote at length, attribute, and ShareAlike any close derivative
published from it).
Order: `0-Pending Acceptance → 1-Discovery → 2-Scoping → 3-Technical Evaluation → 4-Proposal →
5-Negotiating → 6-Awaiting Signature → 7-Closing → Closed Won`, with terminal states `8-Closed
Lost`, `9-Unqualified`, `10-Duplicate`. Exit criteria are concrete and checkable, not aspirational —
for example, `1-Discovery` exits on "recap email sent to customer confirming pain points, key
stakeholders, next steps, project timeline" plus a scoping meeting scheduled, and `3-Technical
Evaluation` exits on demo output shared, ROI measures validated, and the first MEDDPPICC fields
(Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process) populated. This is the exhibit to point to
when the reader asks what a real exit-criteria set looks like — see
[crm-and-pipeline](crm-and-pipeline.md) for why almost no CRM tool can express this structurally
(Odoo is the sole exception among eight surveyed, and ships the field empty by default).

## 2. MEDDPPICC — cite the structure, never reproduce the content

GitLab's own spelling is **MEDDPPICC** — nine letters, two D's, two P's, confirmed against the
page's own nine section headers (`content/handbook/sales/meddppicc.md`,
https://gitlab.com/gitlab-com/content-sites/handbook/-/blob/46180fa3686c8baa55f91bf32c6e1d676eefd475/content/handbook/sales/meddppicc.md,
GitLab handbook, CC BY-SA 4.0) — a distinct variant from the generic MEDDIC/MEDDPICC discussed in
[icp-and-qualification](icp-and-qualification.md), where the frameworks-vs-methods taxonomy and
the MEDDPICC legal ruling (generic for business-education services; summary judgment granted in
part; case live as of 2026-07; McMahon testimony excluded) are the full treatment — this file cites
GitLab's operational instance of the framework, not the ruling. Safe to cite: the nine-letter
expansion and GitLab's own one-line gloss per letter. Not safe to reproduce: the qualifying-question
banks GitLab publishes under each letter — those shade into the trademarked methodology's actual
training content even where GitLab itself published them; paraphrase or omit. MEDDPPICC fields are
gated by ARR threshold ("$100K+ for Enterprise, $50K+ for Mid-Market") — the concrete reason this
overhead belongs here and not on [surface-smb-transactional](surface-smb-transactional.md).

## 3. Sourcegraph's forecast: floor, ceiling, and an openly judged middle

Sourcegraph publishes the forecast method GitLab keeps internal: opportunity-level Won/Commit/Best
Case/Pipeline/Omitted rolling up to Commit/Best Case/**Call**, with its own framing stated
verbatim — "if you consider the Commit as the floor and Best Case as the ceiling, then the Call is
the number in between that you are forecasting as the most likely outcome"
(`content/departments/sales/process/forecasting.md`,
https://github.com/sourcegraph/handbook/blob/5752928b213a3ddbc7cee4459524f404a5b96fb4/content/departments/sales/process/forecasting.md,
Apache-2.0). **This citation does not travel without its staleness caveat: the repo is archived as
of 2024-07-02, and its own content carries Sourcegraph's own warning that it was migrated to Notion
and may be out of date — cite it as "a snapshot of a since-archived handbook," never as current
Sourcegraph practice.** Commit is summed from
records; Call is what a rep judges "will come in" — a published forecast that is explicit its
headline number is a judgment, not a derivation. See
[forecasting-honesty](forecasting-honesty.md) for the flagship treatment; this file cites it
specifically as the enterprise-scale worked exhibit.

## 4. The buying group: the figure never ships without its second clause

Gartner's buying-group figure is accurately quoted wherever it circulates, which is exactly why it
fails the recognise-and-correct test on its own — it only earns its place here attached to the
clause that is routinely dropped: *"the typical buying group for a complex B2B solution involves
six to 10 decision makers, each armed with four or five pieces of information they've gathered
independently and must deconflict with the group"* (Gartner,
`gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey` — the live page 403s every direct fetch; the
quote is verified against a 2020-04-21 Wayback Machine snapshot, not a reconstructed archive.org
URL). The teachable point is
the reconciliation work inside the group, not the headcount. **Ship the figure only with this
second clause attached — the number alone is not the teaching, the deconfliction is.**

## 5. RepVue: quota attainment, always with its subject attached

The best-sourced quota data available anywhere in this pack's research names its own subject:
**share of reps at quota**, not percent-of-quota-attained — thaAdamLittle's RepVue series across
20 large enterprise software companies, Q4-25: median 45%, min 26.7%, max 59.7%
(https://x.com/thaAdamLittle/status/2008940390385152153, self-defines the metric and labels the
rule of thumb explicitly "rough heuristic," the positive model of metric hygiene this pack points
to). Company-specific instances carry the same subject: $SNOW closing 25% bigger deals in 25% less
time with team quota attainment holding at 57% (https://x.com/thaAdamLittle/status/2037542426160648422),
Medallia 19.5%. **No quota-attainment figure attributed to
KBCM/KeyBanc ships anywhere in this pack** — the sibling corpus verified KBCM's subject, not its
value, and no permalink exists. Any bare "70%" attached to quota in this corpus has at least three
distinct subjects across the research — read the subject before citing any figure near this topic.

## 6. The handoff at enterprise scale

The stage-3/4 CSM introduction, the $50K formality threshold, and the "run parallel" sentence that
must travel with any citation of this exhibit are the family seam invariant, not a
surface-specific rule — see [handoff](handoff.md) for the full worked example and the contested
`success` row it sits inside. This surface is where that worked example actually applies: an
enterprise deal is exactly the shape GitLab's exhibit describes (sales-assisted, high-ACV,
multi-role). Do not extend it further than that — it says nothing about lower-touch or CS-native
motions, which is precisely the ground Murphy's contested position occupies.

## Failure modes

- Shipping the "6–10 buying group" figure without its deconfliction clause — the figure alone
  fails the recognise-and-correct test and should not ship on its own.
- Citing RepVue figures without "share of reps at quota" attached, or placing them anywhere near a
  KBCM figure — KBCM ships nowhere in this pack, full stop.
- Citing Sourcegraph's forecast taxonomy without the archived-2024 caveat.
- Reproducing MEDDPPICC's qualifying-question banks at length instead of citing the structure.
- Treating GitLab's stage-gate or CSM-handoff exhibit as evidence that the sales↔success seam is
  settled — it is one worked example inside a contested seam, not an adjudication of it (see
  [handoff](handoff.md)).
- Importing this surface's full MEDDPPICC/stage-gate overhead onto a deal sized for
  [surface-smb-transactional](surface-smb-transactional.md) — GitLab's own ARR gate argues against
  it.
