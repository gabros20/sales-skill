# Discovery Calls

Purpose: State precisely what is evidenced about discovery-call technique and what is not — the
canon conflates "question-asking builds rapport" (real, peer-reviewed) with "our branded questioning
methodology improves win rates" (never published, by anyone). The gap between those two claims is
the teaching.

Read when:
- Choosing or evaluating a discovery-call technique, script, or branded questioning methodology
  (SPIN and its relatives).
- A claim that a questioning methodology "improves win rates" needs a provenance check before it
  ships in a deck, a training doc, or a hire's onboarding.
- Deciding how directive versus conversational a discovery call should be, or how many questions is
  too many.

Skip when:
- The question is a qualification framework's taxonomy, evidence base, or MEDDPICC's legal status
  (MEDDIC, BANT, MEDDPICC) → `icp-and-qualification.md`.
- The question is what happens after discovery — presenting a solution back to the buyer →
  `demo-and-narrative.md`.
- The question is who to call in the first place — ICP, account selection, buying-group mapping →
  `icp-and-qualification.md`, which inherits ICP from marketing (R16); this file assumes the call is
  already booked.
- The question is turning a discovery finding into a battlecard or objection response →
  `objections-and-enablement.md`.
- The question is reading a buyer's stated reason *after* a deal closes → `win-loss-learning.md` —
  this file is about the call itself, that one is about post-hoc explanations.
- The question is product's own "discovery" — continuous user-research, interviews, opportunity
  trees, JTBD → `product/references/discovery.md`. Same English word, unrelated job. This file
  always writes "discovery call" in full; bare "discovery" belongs to product.

Inputs: an upcoming or recent discovery call, or a discovery-call technique/script/methodology being
proposed or evaluated.

Produces: a graded read of what rapport-building question-asking is evidenced to do, a refusal to
let that evidence validate any branded methodology's win-rate claim, and two sourced, adoptable call
mechanics.

## 1. The precise position

Two papers, both PRIMARY, peer-reviewed in *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*:

- Huang, K., Yeomans, M., Brooks, A. W., Minson, J. A., & Gino, F. (2017). "It doesn't hurt to ask:
  Question-asking increases liking." DOI 10.1037/pspi0000097.
- Yeomans, M., Brooks, A. W., Huang, K., Minson, J. A., & Gino, F. (2019). "It helps to ask: The
  cumulative benefits of asking follow-up questions." DOI 10.1037/pspi0000220.

Both establish that asking questions — especially *follow-up* questions — measurably increases how
much a conversational counterpart likes the asker. The caveat is load-bearing and must travel
whenever these are cited: both studies are conducted in **speed-dating and get-to-know-you dyads**,
not sales discovery calls. They show that question-asking builds rapport in conversation generally.
They do not show that any particular questioning structure closes more deals, and they were never
designed to.

The honest, sharper position: **there is good evidence that question-asking builds rapport, and no
published evidence that any branded sales questioning methodology improves win rates.** The canon
routinely conflates these two claims — citing rapport evidence as if it validated a branded method's
commercial outcome. That gap is itself the teachable content.

## 2. What SPIN cannot borrow

SPIN Selling's own claimed evidence base — 35,000 sales calls, 12 years, 20+ countries — is not
published anywhere reviewable. The claim traces to an archived copy of Rackham's own speaker-bureau
bio page, not to a study, technical report, or dataset; Huthwaite's current marketing blog restates
the same figures in Rackham's own words and adds no citation. Grade: VENDOR-SELF-REPORTED, and the
underlying study is a null result for publication.

This matters here specifically because of what it rules out: the JPSP rapport papers in §1 cannot
retroactively supply SPIN (or any other branded questioning method) the validation its own study
never published. Rapport-building and win-rate improvement are different claims, studied by different
people, with different evidence. Never let one stand in for the other.

## 3. What the practitioner surface adds

A first-person account of improving *by discarding* the branded frameworks, not adopting one:

> "I didn't get really good at sales until I learned how to relax during discovery calls. The
> biggest thing that lead to this breakthrough was when I started treating sales calls like
> conversations. I threw MEDDIC, BANT, and frameworks out the window." — @techsaleshackz,
> 2025-12-16, https://x.com/techsaleshackz/status/2000955127813824846

FIRST-PERSON, and the reported outcome is qualitative ("really good at sales"), not a number — it
cannot be weighed against any methodology's own claims, and it is one person's account, not a
comparison. What it is good evidence of is the practitioner *position*: treating the call as a
conversation rather than a script, which is consistent with §1's actual finding (questions build
rapport) without needing a branded label at all.

A second exhibit pushes back directly on the "too many questions burns the call" folk rule:

> "Prospects don't get annoyed that you ask a lot of questions, they get annoyed when they feel you
> aren't really listening." — @BowTiedSalesGuy, 2023-06-13,
> https://x.com/BowTiedSalesGuy/status/1668735943522623488

OPINION, not a study — but it relocates the failure mode from question *volume* to *listening*,
which is a more falsifiable and more actionable frame than "ask fewer questions."

## 4. Two adoptable mechanics, sourced honestly

**Set an objective, not an agenda.**

> "Never want to have your discovery interrupted with, 'you asked for this call - just tell me what
> you do' again? Set an objective, not an agenda." — @KyleAsay_, 2024-04-11,
> https://x.com/KyleAsay_/status/1778512091546099740

OPINION from a vendor-employed author, but the mechanic itself does not depend on the vendor's
product: state what the call should produce by the end, rather than a topic list, so the buyer knows
why they are answering questions.

**Know who is actually in the room.**

> "Toys are marketed to children, but sold to parents. Consider that when you're developing a
> marketing and sales strategy for your SaaS. The people who want to use it and the ones who pay for
> it might sit in very different rooms, speaking a very different corporate language." —
> @arvidkahl, 2023-01-19, https://x.com/arvidkahl/status/1616150668405768218

OPINION. For a solo reader, this is the discovery-call-level version of a buying-group question —
who is on this specific call, user or payer, and which questions that person can actually answer.
The account-level version of this question — mapping a full buying group, ICP fit — is
`icp-and-qualification.md`'s job, not this file's; this file only asks it about the person on the
call in front of you.

## 5. Naming discipline

Per family term discipline: **"discovery call" is written in full on every use in this pack, never
bare "discovery."** `product` owns "discovery" as its own term for continuous user research,
interviews, and opportunity-tree work (`product/references/discovery.md`) — the same English word
doing a different job in a sibling pack. A reader who sees bare "discovery" in a sales context should
read that as a drafting error, not a synonym.

## Validation

- Any rapport-building claim cites Huang 2017 or Yeomans 2019 with the speed-dating/get-to-know-you
  caveat attached, never as free-standing "questions work in sales."
- No branded questioning methodology's win-rate claim is supported by the JPSP papers.
- "Discovery call" is spelled out on every occurrence; no bare "discovery."
- A practitioner quote used as evidence states its grade (FIRST-PERSON / OPINION) and is not treated
  as a comparison or a study.

## Failure modes and handoff

- **The JPSP rapport papers are cited to validate a branded methodology's win-rate claim.** Apply
  §§1–2 — refuse the substitution and state the actual, narrower finding.
- **A single discard anecdote (techsaleshackz) is read as "frameworks don't work."** Apply §3 — it is
  one qualitative first-person account, not a comparison against any framework's own claims.
- **"Discovery" appears bare in a sales context.** Apply §5 — disambiguate against
  `product/references/discovery.md` and correct to "discovery call."
- **The question turns out to be about qualification frameworks or MEDDPICC's trademark status.**
  Route to `icp-and-qualification.md`.
- **The question turns out to be about reading a closed deal's stated loss or win reason.** Route to
  `win-loss-learning.md`.
- **A discovery finding needs to become a battlecard entry.** Route to
  `objections-and-enablement.md`.
