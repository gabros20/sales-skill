## 1. The practitioner verdict on named methodologies: mostly rejection

**@techsaleshackz** · 2025-12-16 · https://x.com/techsaleshackz/status/2000955127813824846 · **FIRST-PERSON — strongest methodology critique found**
> "I didn't get really good at sales until I learned how to relax during discovery calls. The biggest
> thing that lead to this breakthrough was when I started treating sales calls like conversations. I
> threw MEDDIC, BANT, and frameworks out the window. I sat there in my chair & st…"

A first-person account of improving *by discarding* the frameworks. Note the reported outcome is
qualitative ("really good at sales") — not a number — so it cannot be weighed against the frameworks'
own claims. But it is the clearest statement of the practitioner position, and it is the single most
useful exhibit in this file.

**@BowTiedSalesGuy** · 2022-06-30 · https://x.com/BowTiedSalesGuy/status/1542578172188819456 · OPINION
> "BANT MAN FAB MEDDIC / NGMI."

**@TechSalesGuy** · 2026-03-11 · https://x.com/TechSalesGuy/status/2031522097302786500 · **OPINION — acronym proliferation**
> "sales acronyms keep getting longer / BANT SPICED MEDDIC FAVORITE / I dare someone to build one
> that spells QUOTA ATTAINMENT"

Worth citing for the structural point rather than the joke: the acronym set keeps *growing*, which is
what a field looks like when nothing gets falsified and retired. That is precisely d1's finding
(SPIN's evidentiary base is a NULL, BANT is folklore) observed from the practitioner side.

**@TechSalesGuy** · 2026-01-17 · https://x.com/TechSalesGuy/status/2012569476659597805 · OPINION (derisive)

Important nuance: the mockery targets *framework collecting*, while endorsing **qualification itself**

vocabulary the reader will encounter, and reproduce none of them.

## 2. Practitioners minting their own frameworks (the supply side of the problem)

**@BrianLaManna_** · 2026-03-01 · https://x.com/BrianLaManna_/status/2028093178490900713 · **OPINION — explicit non-MEDDIC framing**
> "6 non-negotiables for me to uncover in every sales cycle. Without it, you're likely to lose often
> to 'no decision.' And no - this is not MEDDIC. It's the concept of 'why change' and why an
> investment is required. 1. 𝐈𝐝𝐞𝐧𝐭𝐢𝐟𝐲 𝐚 𝐜𝐨𝐦𝐩𝐚𝐧𝐲-𝐬𝐩𝐞𝐜𝐢𝐟𝐢𝐜 𝐨𝐛𝐣𝐞𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐯𝐞 How to ask: ↳ 'Gong …"

Note the explicit disclaimer "this is not MEDDIC" — a practitioner actively distancing his own
checklist from the trademarked one. Two things for the pack: (a) "loss to **no decision**" as the
primary failure mode, which is a better teaching target than losing to a competitor; (b) "**why
change**" as the root question, converging with axtalks's "why now" in b2 from a completely
different direction.

**@FidelCacheFlow** · 2026-06-17 · https://x.com/FidelCacheFlow/status/2067366055102615697 · **FOLKLORE — how a methodology gets minted**
> "HPP methodology is a culmination of 7 years of my personal selling experience in 2x President's
> Club and $19m ARR sold along with collaboration from a 5x President's Club Strat AE rep at my
> former org, my personal sales coach that closed half a billion in software across two deca…"

Why this is valuable beyond its content: it is a **live specimen of methodology genesis**. The stated
evidence base is personal tenure and awards — "7 years", "2x President's Club", "$19m ARR sold",
"half a billion closed" — none of which is auditable, and none of which is a test of the method. This
is exactly how d1 found SPIN's and BANT's bases to be non-traceable. Cite it as the pattern, not the
method.

## 3. Discovery and the buying side

**@KyleAsay_** · 2024-04-11 · https://x.com/KyleAsay_/status/1778512091546099740 · **OPINION — concrete discovery mechanic**
> "Never want to have your discovery interrupted with, 'you asked for this call - just tell me what
> you do' again? Set an objective, not an agenda. Here's what that could look like for a Gong rep
> selling to a VP of sales: 'By the end of the call today, I'll be able to share how w…"

The most directly adoptable discovery technique found on X — objective-over-agenda, with a worked
example. Vendor-employed author, but the mechanic is not product-dependent.

**@BowTiedSalesGuy** · 2023-06-13 · https://x.com/BowTiedSalesGuy/status/1668735943522623488 · OPINION
> "Prospects don't get annoyed that you ask a lot of questions, they get annoyed when they feel you
> aren't really listening. Can you blame them? They just told you they're looking to get promoted and
> why, and all you do is nod and say 'Absolutely' before asking another unrelated qu…"

Directly contradicts the "too many questions burns the call" folk rule; relocates the failure to
listening. Useful counterweight when the pack writes discovery guidance.

**@KyleAsay_** · 2024-12-12 · https://x.com/KyleAsay_/status/1867256294828101875 · **OPINION — buyer-side inversion**
> "If you can answer this one question, you'll meaningfully improve every stage of the deal cycle:
> 'What's the #1 email/call your buyer does not want to receive?' Examples: VP Sales - 'how did you
> miss forecast this badly?' VP Marketing - 'there's no evidence that marketing spen…"

Buying motivation framed as the buyer's *personal* risk rather than the company's business case.
Cheap, memorable, and it needs no framework vocabulary.

**@arvidkahl** · 2023-01-19 · https://x.com/arvidkahl/status/1616150668405768218 · **OPINION — buying committee without the jargon**
> "Toys are marketed to children, but sold to parents. Consider that when you're developing a
> marketing and sales strategy for your SaaS. The people who want to use it and the ones who pay for
> it might sit in very different rooms, speaking a very different corporate language."

(Also in b1.) The user≠buyer split in one image. For a solo reader this beats "buying committee"
terminology, and it carries the marketing/sales seam inside it.

**@Shpigford** · 2026-06-16 · https://x.com/Shpigford/status/2066881769027047658 · OPINION (also b1)
> "if you still require an enterprise sales call to give pricing, you're going to be eaten alive…"

The buying side refusing the gated process altogether — the buyer-side counterpart to the existence gate.

**@alexabelonix** · 2026-08-02 · https://x.com/alexabelonix/status/2083908984629502173 · FIRST-PERSON RESULT (also b1)
> "…booked 4 calls = around 8% conversion … > changing my ICP > adding new features > clarifying the
> positioning"

Discovery calls used to revise ICP — qualification as a learning loop rather than a filter.

## 4. Objection handling and negotiation

**@BrianLaManna_** · 2025-09-30 · https://x.com/BrianLaManna_/status/1972968791828000962 · **FIRST-PERSON RESULT — late-stage negotiation**
> "Got a massive deal out there with a 9/30 close date. After we got asked for our best & final
> twice, they are now trying to negotiate a 3rd time, with a new person. Hardest thing to do in sales
> but you have to know your drop dead number & what thresholds cannot be crossed."

A real end-of-quarter squeeze: repeated best-and-final, a new negotiator introduced late, against a
quarter-end close date. Two teachables — pre-committed walk-away thresholds, and the fact that the
seller's own close-date pressure is the lever being used against him. Ties the negotiation section to
the forecasting section (b3): the forecast date *creates* the vulnerability.

**@BrianLaManna_** · 2026-03-29 · https://x.com/BrianLaManna_/status/2038250214230814796 · OPINION (also b3)
> "When I have a surplus of pipeline: -Disqualifying properly -Keeping stronger price integrity
> -Holding the line on our buyer process"

Pipeline volume as the precondition for discounting discipline — the structural answer to "how do I
stop discounting", and it is a pipeline fix, not a scripting fix.

**@patio11** · 2026-06-02 · https://x.com/patio11/status/2061910386966729182 · OPINION (also b1)
> "'Don't do this if isn't the right deal for you: here are some reasons it might not be.'"

Negative selling that works even when disclosed as a tactic.

## 5. The "57% of the buying journey" class of claim — X-side result

**Searched: `buying journey before contacting`. Result: NULL on this surface.** The query returned
no sales-related discourse at all (results were consumer-electronics, crypto and e-commerce noise).

This is a genuine finding rather than a failed query, and it should be recorded as such:
- The **57%/"most of the buying decision happens before sales contact"** claim is heavily discussed
  in *vendor and analyst* material (d1/d2's ground, plus Channel E's note that webdesign carried a
  second instance of the genre: "9 in 10 buyers rule out before contact").
- It does **not** circulate as practitioner conversation on X under this phrasing.
- **Implication for the pack:** the claim is a *content-marketing artifact*, not practitioner
  folklore. That changes the correction strategy — the reader will meet it in a vendor deck or a blog
  post, not from a colleague, so the pack should teach recognition-in-published-material rather than
  positioning it as a myth peers repeat.
- ⚠️ **Caveat on this null:** only ONE phrasing was tried, and the crew-wide WebSearch outage means I
  could not cross-check via a web surface. Before the pack asserts "practitioners don't repeat this",
  re-run with `57%`, `buyer's journey`, `before they contact sales`, and `Gartner buying group`.

   over time. The pack must not claim a consensus in either direction. The original null was produced
   by querying only `MEDDIC` — the acronym most used derisively — which selected for dismissal.
