## 1. SPIN Selling (Neil Rackham / Huthwaite)

### Origin
Neil Rackham founded Huthwaite Research Group in 1974 (UK), later Huthwaite Inc. (Northern Virginia) and Huthwaite Ltd. He led it until 2003, then became a strategic advisor. He holds a psychology degree from Sheffield University (1966) and was a post-graduate research fellow there through 1969; later held visiting-professor titles at Portsmouth, Cranfield, Cincinnati, and Sheffield. *SPIN Selling* was published in 1988 (McGraw-Hill).
Source: [Neil Rackham — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Rackham) — GRADE: SECONDARY (encyclopedia synthesis, but traceable).

### Evidence claim
The canonical claim, repeated across virtually every vendor/summary source: a team of researchers studied ~35,000 sales calls, in 20+ countries, over 12 years, funded in part by ~$1 million from companies including Xerox and IBM, coded against ~116 behavioral variables, concluding that "SPIN" questions (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff) characterize high performers in complex/large sales.

**Load-bearing finding: the Wikipedia claim of "35,000 calls / 30 researchers / 20 countries / 12 years / $1M funding" is NOT footnoted to any journal, technical report, or dataset.** I fetched the Wikipedia page directly and inspected its citation list: that specific claim is sourced to Reference #8, which is an **archived version of Rackham's own personal-website bio page**:
> "Bio | Neil Rackham - Sales Speaker, Leading Authority on Consultative Selling" — https://web.archive.org/web/20190420125009/http://neilrackham.com/bio/
GRADE: VENDOR-SELF-REPORTED (a speaker-bureau bio, not research documentation).

Wikipedia's Reference #11, which does cite the *Journal of Personal Selling & Sales Management*, turns out on inspection to be a **book review** of Rackham's *Managing Major Sales*, not a methodology paper or dataset release — it does not describe or validate the underlying 35,000-call study.

Huthwaite International's own current marketing blog, when asked directly, could not fill the gap either. I fetched https://www.huthwaiteinternational.com/blog/neil-rackham-research-spin directly and it:
- confirms scale in Rackham's own words ("watch 35,000 calls in real life," 20 sales managers each coaching 3 reps over 3 months, companies named: Xerox, IBM, Motorola)
- gives a results figure ("first thousand people trained using the SPIN model against control groups had a 17% higher sales volume")
- gives **no dates, no country count, no funding figure, and — critically — no citation to any peer-reviewed publication, technical report, or accessible dataset.**
GRADE: VENDOR-SELF-REPORTED.

### Evidence grade + verdict
**NULL RESULT, stated explicitly: the underlying 35,000-call/12-year SPIN study is not published anywhere reviewable that this search could locate.** No Journal of Personal Selling & Sales Management (JPSSM) paper, no Huthwaite technical report, no dataset, no methodology appendix beyond the 1988 book and secondary retellings of it. Rackham does have a genuine peer-reviewed publication record (*Industrial and Commercial Training*, 1972; *Journal of European Industrial Training*, 1978 (two parts); pieces in *Harvard Business Review*, *McKinsey Quarterly*, *Journal of Product Innovation Management*) — but nothing in what I could fetch ties any of those specific citations to the SPIN call-coding study itself; they appear to be a broader career bibliography, not the SPIN evidentiary trail.

Search paths tried and exhausted: Wikipedia + its reference list (traced to a personal bio page, not a study); Huthwaite International's own blog on the research (no citation); ResearchGate site-restricted search for Rackham SPIN papers (surfaced only papers that *apply* the SPIN model to other domains — e.g., a car-buyer marketing-communication study — never the original Huthwaite study itself, and one hit dated the model "Rackham, 1996" vs. the book's actual 1988 publication, a minor secondary-source inconsistency worth flagging); Semantic Scholar API (rate-limited/429 on this attempt, not independently confirmed clean).

### Best published critique
Could not locate a dedicated JPSSM or Industrial Marketing Management critique paper specifically interrogating the SPIN study's methodology (as exists for Challenger — see §2). The closest located, at review level rather than primary critique, is general commentary noting skepticism about the sheer logistics of "a research team watching 35,000 calls in real life" — this was itself a secondary paraphrase inside a WebFetch summary, not a citable named source, so it is NOT included as a citable finding, only flagged as a direction. Treat "no substantive published academic critique of the SPIN evidence base was found" as its own null result.

### IP + trademark
Two separate live trademark threads found via Justia:
- **SPIN SELLING** — registered to **Korn Ferry (US)**, Reg. No. 2413866, filed September 1999. Source: https://trademarks.justia.com/758/04/spin-75804310.html — GRADE: PRIMARY (trademark registry record). Korn Ferry holds this because it acquired Miller Heiman Group in 2019, whose portfolio included a licensed/related "SPIN Selling Conversations" training product — Miller Heiman was NOT Huthwaite; this is a second, separate commercial lineage for the SPIN name, not the same company as Rackham's Huthwaite.
- **HUTHWAITE CREATORS OF SPIN SELLING** — Reg. No. 2823457, owner Huthwaite Research Group Limited (filed July 1978 per the underlying SPIN mark lineage). Source: https://trademarks.justia.com/782/39/huthwaite-creators-of-spin-selling-78239391.html — GRADE: PRIMARY.
- Per the Wikipedia summary of a 2003 federal case (*Huthwaite, Inc. v. Sunrise Assisted Living, Inc.*, 261 F. Supp. 2d 502, E.D. Va. 2003), Huthwaite has litigated to enforce SPIN-related marks before. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/261/502/2515576/ — GRADE: PRIMARY (case exists; I did not fetch full opinion text, only the case listing).
- Per WebFetch summary of trademark search results: in May 2000, Rackham assigned to Huthwaite his retained rights in the SPIN Selling copyright — GRADE: SECONDARY (summarized from trademark-database commentary, not independently verified against a primary assignment document).

**Practical takeaway for the skill pack: "SPIN Selling" today sits under at least two different corporate umbrellas (Huthwaite International, independent; and Korn Ferry, via the Miller Heiman acquisition) — do not assume a single current owner.**

## 2. The Challenger Sale (Matthew Dixon & Brent Adamson, CEB)

### Origin
Published November 10, 2011 (Portfolio/Penguin), by Matthew Dixon and Brent Adamson, based on research conducted at CEB Inc. (Corporate Executive Board). CEB was acquired by Gartner in 2017 (referenced across multiple vendor/secondary sources located; I did not independently pull a Gartner press release for the exact 2017 date/terms in this pass — treat the year as SECONDARY, widely and consistently reported but not primary-source-verified here).
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Challenger_Sale — GRADE: SECONDARY. Note: the Wikipedia article itself carries a "needs more citations" maintenance banner and does **not** contain the sample-size, methodology, or win-rate statistics — those numbers had to be corroborated from vendor/summary sources instead (see below), which is itself notable: even Wikipedia's editors flagged this article as under-sourced.

### Evidence claim
Widely repeated claim: a study of ~6,000 sales reps across ~90 companies, sorting reps into 5 profiles (Hard Worker, Relationship Builder, Lone Wolf, Reactive Problem Solver [not independently confirmed by name in what was fetched], Challenger), with Challengers being disproportionately represented among top performers — commonly cited as ~39% of top performers vs. ~27% of the overall rep population (this specific 39%/27% pairing came from a vendor secondary source, not from CEB/Dixon/Adamson primary material fetched directly — GRADE: VENDOR-SELF-REPORTED / SECONDARY, unverified against a primary CEB report in this pass).

### Evidence grade + verdict
Unlike SPIN, the Challenger Sale claims **did** attract a formal academic review — this is the strongest example in this whole methodology set of a named methodology getting real peer-reviewed scrutiny.

**Rapp, A., Bachrach, D. G., Panagopoulos, N., & Ogilvie, J. (2014). "Salespeople as knowledge brokers: A review and critique of the challenger sales model." *Journal of Personal Selling & Sales Management*, 34(4), 245–259.**
- Metadata confirmed via Crossref API (https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/08853134.2014.908126) — GRADE: PRIMARY (bibliographic record).
- Confirmed as the 2014 Marvin Jolson Award winner for Best Contribution to Selling and Sales Management Practice in JPSSM, and listed among the most-downloaded Routledge Social Science articles of 2014. Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08853134.2014.908126 (abstract page metadata fetched via search snippet; full-text page itself returned HTTP 403 to WebFetch, so I could NOT read the abstract text directly — flagging this as unverified full content, verified only at the metadata/title/award level).
- A quote surfaced from the article's framing (via search snippet, not full-text fetch, so treat as lower-confidence): *"Although generating tension with customers has received a great deal of interest, researchers have yet to complete a systematic, in-depth examination of the Challenger model."* — this framing sentence is the paper's stated rationale for existing, i.e., it positions itself explicitly as filling an evidence gap CEB's own materials had not filled.

**Correction to the brief's expected author names:** the task brief suggested searching for "Rapp, Beeler, Dugan" as Challenger critics. The actual, Crossref-confirmed author list of the主 JPSSM critique paper is **Rapp, Bachrach, Panagopoulos, and Ogilvie** — I could not find a separate Beeler/Dugan-authored Challenger critique in JPSSM or Industrial Marketing Management in the searching done here (DuckDuckGo-substitute search for "Beeler Dugan Rapp Challenger Sale critique" returned only the Rapp/Bachrach/Panagopoulos/Ogilvie paper and noted Lisa Beeler's separate, unrelated salesperson-brand-relationship research). Treat "a Beeler/Dugan-specific Challenger critique" as a **null result** in this search — it may exist but was not located.

I could **not** fetch the original CEB/Dixon-Adamson 6,000-rep study itself as a standalone citable primary document (no independent Gartner/CEB technical-report URL located and fetched) — the sample-size and 5-profile claims are corroborated only by the book's marketing apparatus and third-party vendor retellings, not by a primary research report I could independently verify. This is a secondary evidence gap parallel to (though less severe than) the SPIN situation, since at least the *book* itself is the primary artifact here and an independent academic party did engage with it critically — SPIN never got that.

### Best critique
Rapp, Bachrach, Panagopoulos & Ogilvie (2014), JPSSM 34(4):245-259, above — GRADE for the critique's existence: PRIMARY (a real, awarded, peer-reviewed journal article exists). GRADE for its specific content as reproduced here: SECONDARY/LOW-CONFIDENCE, because the full text was not fetchable (403) and I'm relying on the DOI/Crossref metadata plus a search-engine snippet framing sentence.

### IP + trademark
CEB → Gartner (2017 acquisition, per multiple consistent secondary sources, not independently primary-verified in this pass). "Challenger," "Challenger Sale," and the associated typology are commercialized today by **Challenger Inc.** (challengerinc.com), a Gartner spinout/licensee — I did not pull a specific USPTO trademark record for "Challenger Sale" in this pass; treat trademark specifics as unverified (not fabricated — simply not fetched).

## 3. MEDDIC / MEDDICC / MEDDPICC

### Origin
Primary-adjacent account, from meddicc.com's own "Who Created MEDDIC" page (fetched directly): **Dick Dunkel created MEDDIC in 1996 at PTC (Parametric Technology Corporation)**, working under SVP **John McMahon**, in collaboration with **Jack Napoli**. Context given: PTC's sales org faced severe attrition (~300 people), and Dunkel/Napoli were tasked with analyzing past deals to find repeatable patterns; they identified six recurring factors: **M**etrics, **E**conomic Buyer, **D**ecision Criteria, **D**ecision Process, **I**dentify Pain, **C**hampion.
Source: https://meddicc.com/resources/who-created-meddic — GRADE: VENDOR-SELF-REPORTED (this is MEDDICC Ltd's own resources page, not an independent historical account, though it names real, checkable individuals).

**Important date discrepancy found:** A separate primary-grade source — a 2026 U.S. federal court opinion (see below) — describes MEDDPICC (the extended acronym) as "coined in 2005 by former Parametric Technology Corporation (PTC) employees and built on the earlier MEDDIC acronym developed at PTC **in the early 1990s**" — i.e., the court's framing places original MEDDIC slightly earlier than the commonly repeated "1996" date. This is a genuine, source-attested inconsistency in the record, not a search failure — flag it as CONTESTED on the exact year (early-1990s vs. 1996).

### Evidence claim
No empirical performance study is claimed by any source located — MEDDIC's evidentiary basis, per the vendor's own account, is "analysis of hundreds of PTC's past deals," an internal, non-published pattern analysis, plus the anecdotal/vendor-repeated claim that "PTC grew annual revenue from $300 million to over $1 billion in four years" using MEDDIC (found via search-engine summary of vendor content, NOT independently fetched from a primary PTC financial filing — GRADE: VENDOR-SELF-REPORTED, unverified against SEC/financial records in this pass).

### Evidence grade + verdict
**NULL RESULT: no peer-reviewed or otherwise independently published empirical study of MEDDIC was located.** A targeted search (DuckDuckGo-substitute, "MEDDIC empirical study peer reviewed academic journal win rate") returned only sales-software vendor guides and consulting blogs, plus one entirely unrelated medical-journal hit (cardiac efficacy ratios — a false-positive keyword match, explicitly not relevant, noted here only to show the search was actually attempted and came up empty rather than unsearched). Commonly repeated performance figures like "18 percent higher win rates" or "20–30% higher win rates" appear in vendor marketing content (e.g., a company called Bliro) with **no citation to an underlying study** — these are exactly the kind of number that should not be repeated as fact by the skill pack (see Never-Ship Figures below).
GRADE for the whole methodology: FOLKLORE-adjacent / VENDOR-SELF-REPORTED — practitioner-transmitted lineage with named, checkable originators, but zero independent empirical validation located.

### Best critique
No dedicated academic critique paper of MEDDIC was located in this search (unlike Challenger). This is itself a null result worth stating: MEDDIC, despite being arguably the most widely deployed enterprise qualification framework today, appears to have attracted essentially no academic attention (positive or critical) in the sources reachable here.

### IP + trademark — the most significant finding in this section
**A U.S. federal court just ruled on this, days before this research (which lands inside this same news cycle).** Fetched directly from PR Newswire UK:
- **MEDDICC Ltd v. Darius Lahoutifard** (d/b/a 01 Consulting LLC / "MEDDIC Academy"), U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania, Civil Action No. 24-1836.
- Chief Judge Wendy Beetlestone granted summary judgment on **April 21, 2026**, ordering the USPTO to **cancel Lahoutifard's trademark registration No. 6,489,058** for "MEDDPICC" — the court held **MEDDPICC is a generic term** naming a sales methodology category, not a protectable brand.
- Direct quote from the court's opinion as reproduced in the release: Lahoutifard "was not involved in MEDDPICC's conception," yet "sixteen years after its genesis, he federally registered the term as his trademark" — the "sixteen years after genesis" framing, combined with the 2026 ruling date, implies a ~2005 origin for the specific MEDDPICC extension (consistent with the "coined in 2005" framing elsewhere in the same release).
- The court's opinion states MEDDPICC "coined in 2005 by former Parametric Technology Corporation (PTC) employees and built on the earlier MEDDIC acronym developed at PTC in the early 1990s."
- The court relied in part on expert testimony from **John McMahon** — the same PTC SVP named as the supervising executive in MEDDIC's original 1996(ish) creation story — who is described as "directly involved in the development of both MEDDIC and MEDDPICC," giving this ruling unusual evidentiary weight as closer to a primary-source-adjudicated history than typical vendor lore.
- All of Lahoutifard's counterclaims were dismissed with prejudice.
Source: https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/us-federal-court-rules-meddpicc-is-a-generic-term-orders-cancellation-of-trademark-registration-302751623.html — GRADE: PRIMARY (court ruling reported via press release; I did not independently pull the PACER docket/opinion PDF, so grade the *existence and outcome* of the ruling as primary-adjacent/high-confidence, but the exact opinion text as SECONDARY since I only read the PR Newswire characterization, not the opinion itself).
- Also independently corroborated at IPWatchdog: https://ipwatchdog.com/press/us-federal-court-rules-meddpicc-is-a-generic-term-orders-cancellation-of-trademark-registration/ (same underlying release, not independently fetched in this pass but the mirror listing corroborates the story is real and not a single-outlet artifact).

**Implication for the skill pack: as of April 2026, "MEDDPICC" is legally a generic descriptive term in the U.S. (post trademark cancellation), while "MEDDICC" remains under MEDDICC Ltd (Andy Whyte, founded 2020) commercially. "MEDDIC" itself was never shown in this research to be currently trademark-registered to anyone — treat the base acronym as public-domain-style descriptive language, consistent with the court's generic-term finding for its extended sibling.**

## 4. Sandler Selling System (David Sandler, 1967)

### Origin
David Sandler founded Sandler Training/the Sandler Selling System in 1967; franchised the training model starting 1983 as the Sandler Sales Institute. The "Submarine" metaphor for the seven-step process is attributed to Sandler's fondness for WWII submarine movies — the operating idea being that each step of a sale must be "sealed" before moving to the next, the way a submarine seals compartments against flooding.
Sources (secondary, vendor/consumer content, consistent across multiple independent vendor retellings but no primary Sandler-company historical archive independently fetched): dock.us, gong.io, johnnygrow.com summaries — GRADE: SECONDARY, converging but not primary-verified.

### Evidence claim
No empirical study claim was located for the Sandler Selling System at all — unlike SPIN or Challenger, it does not appear to make a "we studied N calls" claim in the first place. Its legitimacy claim is business-model-based (a 200+-location franchise network operating in 34+ countries) rather than research-based.

### Evidence grade + verdict
**GRADE: FOLKLORE / no evidence claim to evaluate.** This is a different category of null result from SPIN's: SPIN makes a big empirical claim that turns out unpublished; Sandler makes essentially no empirical claim at all — it's presented purely as a training/franchise methodology, sealing off the standard of evidence question entirely. Worth stating explicitly in the skill pack: Sandler should not be described as "research-backed" in any sense — no such claim was made or found.

### Best critique
No published academic critique located. No search was fruitful for one dedicated specifically to Sandler methodology in JPSSM or similar journals within this pass's budget.

### IP + trademark
**Sandler Systems, Inc.** — incorporated in Maryland, November 15, 1983, principal address 300 Red Brook Boulevard, Suite 400, Owings Mills, MD. Does business as "Sandler" and "Sandler Training." Franchisor entity for the training/franchise network. Source: aggregated from franchise-disclosure-document-derived vendor listings (franchise.com, franchisedirect.com, thefranchisebuilders.com summaries) — GRADE: SECONDARY (franchise-directory data, plausible and internally consistent, not independently pulled from a primary FDD or SEC filing in this pass).

## 5. BANT (attributed to IBM)

### Origin — this is a confirmed FOLKLORE-ORIGIN finding
The task brief predicted this would likely be undocumented folklore. **That prediction is confirmed.**

- Vendor sources uniformly assert "BANT was first used at IBM," with the specific decade varying by source — some say the 1950s, some the 1960s, with **no source giving a specific year, document, executive name, or citable primary IBM record.** Example located and directly fetched: Mailchimp's resource page states flatly "The BANT sales framework was first used at IBM in the 1950s" with **zero citation** for that claim. Source: https://mailchimp.com/resources/what-is-bant/ — GRADE: FOLKLORE (confident assertion, no support).
- **Wikipedia has no article for "BANT"** — direct fetch of https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BANT returned HTTP 404. For a claimed 60-70-year-old, IBM-originated, globally taught framework, the absence of even a stub Wikipedia article is itself notable circumstantial evidence that the standard attribution has never been rigorously sourced by anyone maintaining that kind of record.
- I attempted a search specifically for IBM-primary documentation (IBM developerWorks, IBM archives, any dated internal sales manual) via DuckDuckGo-substitute search. It surfaced no IBM archival material, no developerWorks page, and no dated citation — only the same undocumented secondary/vendor attribution repeated across dozens of sales-tech blog posts (Pipedrive, Salesfully, Sybill, Informa TechTarget, LinkedIn posts, Salesmotion, Supered, Ampliz, Unbound B2B, LeadGenius — all surveyed via snippet, none offering a primary citation).

### Evidence grade + verdict
**GRADE: FOLKLORE.** The IBM origin of BANT is an oft-repeated, zero-citation attribution. No primary IBM document, archive entry, dated internal manual, or named IBM individual was found anywhere in this search. Treat "BANT was invented by IBM" as an unsupported claim the skill pack should either omit or explicitly caveat as "widely but unverifiably attributed to IBM."

### Best published critique
Real and substantive practitioner-level critique exists (not academic-journal-grade, but consistent and specific) around BANT's **budget-first premise being mismatched to modern buying behavior**: multiple sources converge on the point that BANT assumes a pre-existing, centralized budget and single decision-maker, whereas modern enterprise software buying (subscription model, 6–10 typical stakeholders) routinely has buyers identify a preferred vendor **before** a budget line exists — one source cited a figure that "over 70% of enterprise software buyers identify a preferred vendor before an official procurement line item or formal budget allocation even exists" (GRADE: VENDOR-SELF-REPORTED / uncredited figure — the specific 70%+ number traces to no named study in what was fetched; treat as an unsupported statistic, not a citable fact). Proposed alternative sequencing found: reversing the framework into "TNAB" (Timeframe, Need, Authority, Budget). Source aggregation: https://www.informatechtarget.com/blog/why-bant-fails-for-modern-enterprise-technology-demand-generation/ and similar (surveyed via search summary, not independently fetched line-by-line in this pass).

### IP + trademark
No trademark registration for "BANT" was located or searched to conclusion in this pass — consistent with it functioning as an unowned, generic descriptive acronym in the market (nobody appears to assert ownership over it, unlike MEDDIC/MEDDPICC where ownership was actively litigated).

### Links

## 6. Gap Selling (Keenan / A Sales Guy)

### Origin
Created by **Jim "Keenan" [surname used professionally as "Keenan"]**, CEO of A Sales Guy Inc., introduced in his 2018 book *Gap Selling: Getting the Customer to Yes*. Core concept: frame the sales conversation around the "gap" between a prospect's current state and desired future state — a problem-centric (vs. relationship- or feature-centric) discovery approach. Keenan has since published *Gap Prospecting* (with Will Aitken) and has a further book (*Gap Revenue Performance*) referenced as forthcoming. Source: aggregated vendor/book-retailer listings (Amazon, Goodreads, salesgrowth.com) — GRADE: SECONDARY.

### Evidence claim
None located. No study, sample size, or dataset is claimed anywhere in the material surveyed — the book's authority rests on Keenan's own consulting/sales-leadership experience and practitioner testimonial, not on a research claim.

### Evidence grade + verdict
**GRADE: FOLKLORE / no evidence claim made.** Same category as Sandler: there is no empirical claim to evaluate, so there's nothing to debunk — but the skill pack should not imply Gap Selling has a research basis, because none was located or claimed. Practitioner sentiment surveyed (e.g., informal community discussion) was mixed-positive ("good principles, problem-centric... doesn't work for everyone," "overkill for transactional or procurement-led deals") but this is anecdotal community commentary, not a citable critique — GRADE: FOLKLORE-level, not included as a hard citation.

### Best critique
None located at any serious/published level.

### IP + trademark
Not independently searched to conclusion in this pass (budget-constrained); no claim made either way — treat as unverified rather than absent.

## 7. Other methodologies — brief

**Miller Heiman Strategic Selling** — Founded on the "Strategic Selling"/"Conceptual Selling" methodologies; the umbrella company (Miller Heiman Group, itself a prior merger of Miller Heiman and AchieveGlobal-adjacent entities) was **acquired by Korn Ferry in 2019** for ~$110M, alongside AchieveForum and Strategy Execution, from TwentyEighty, Inc.; deal announced Sept 30 2019, completed Nov 1 2019. Now operates as part of "Korn Ferry Sell." Its portfolio also historically included a licensed "SPIN Selling Conversations" product — i.e., Korn Ferry's SPIN-related trademark (see §1) likely traces through this acquisition, not through Huthwaite. Source: multiple consistent press releases (Korn Ferry IR page, pulse2.com, MarketScreener) — GRADE: SECONDARY, consistent across independent outlets, not independently pulled from the Korn Ferry IR primary release text in this pass. No empirical study claim was searched for Miller Heiman specifically in this pass.

**Solution Selling (Michael Bosworth)** — Created 1988 by Michael Bosworth; premised on reps diagnosing a specific customer problem before proposing a fix. Bosworth later also developed "CustomerCentric Selling." No empirical study claim located or searched to conclusion. GRADE: SECONDARY (origin only).

**Command of the Message (Force Management)** — Trademark **confirmed via direct fetch**: owned by **Force Management, LLC**; USPTO filing Oct 9, 2009 (Serial #77845503), registered March 8, 2011 (Registration #3929561), Class 35 (advertising/business/retail services), last renewal activity noted as of May 21, 2020. GRADE: PRIMARY (trademark record). Company based Charlotte, NC. No empirical study claim located.

**Value Selling (ValueSelling Associates)** — Founded 1991, Carlsbad CA, led by Julie Thomas (President/CEO); methodology framed as aligning seller process to executive buying decisions, building on consultative-selling principles. GRADE: SECONDARY, aggregated from vendor/company listings, not independently primary-verified.

**NEAT** — Developed by **Richard Harris (Harris Consulting Group)** in collaboration with **Sales Hacker**, mid-2010s, explicitly positioned as a BANT successor. Need, Economic Impact, Access to Authority, Timeline. Source: https://theharrisconsultinggroup.com/neat-selling/ (listed via search, not independently fetched in full) — GRADE: SECONDARY.

**CHAMP** — Attributed to **InsightSquared**, explicitly created "as a direct answer to BANT" by reordering qualification to start with business Challenges rather than Budget. GRADE: SECONDARY, single-source attribution, not independently corroborated against a second source in this pass.

**SPICED (Winning by Design)** — Developed by **Winning by Design**, led by **Jacco van der Kooij**, late 2010s, purpose-built for recurring-revenue (subscription/SaaS) businesses: Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical Event, Decision. GRADE: SECONDARY, vendor-consistent, not independently fetched from a Winning by Design primary methodology page in this pass.

**JOLT Effect (Dixon & McKenna, 2022)** — Book: *The JOLT Effect: How High Performers Overcome Customer Indecision* (Dixon & McKenna, Portfolio/Penguin, 2022).
