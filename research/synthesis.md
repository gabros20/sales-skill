## 1. Wedge — adjudicated

**Verdict (A, adopted): OPEN BY FRAGMENTATION + ADOPTION COLLAPSE, contested on specific items.
Novelty is NOT available.** Several packs ship 9–13 of our 14 canonical items, some well; none has
adoption. The packs with adoption don't cover the domain. Breadth and installs are inversely
correlated. **No pack combines coverage + depth + adoption — that triple is the opening.**

**The one-line wedge (C's formulation, A-adopted):** *the largest incumbent occupies
commercial-finance sales; the buying conversation and the deal system are thin at every scale; and
the accuracy-measurement null survives even the best incumbent.*

Four planks, each multi-channel:

- **P1 — Evidence beneath the posture.** The genre has adopted the FORM of citation (references/
  dirs, "Sources:" lines, named authorities) without the FUNCTION: the best incumbent's
  11,105-line commercial corpus has 30 unique URLs and zero permalinks to any figure, and its

## 2. Flagship — forecasting-and-pipeline honesty, REFRAMED

**The assumption-disclosure posture ("name your assumptions or the forecast is theatre") is PRIOR
ART** — alirezarezvani commercial-forecaster v2.8.0, cite it, never claim novelty. What is OURS:

1. **The evidence line.** Lawrence et al. 2006 (sales "made sure it happened that way" — forecasts
   gamed to target; sandbagging is peer-reviewed, not folklore) + Fildes & Goodwin 2007 (only
   52.8% of professional forecasters ever check whether their own adjustments improve accuracy;
   25% use no error measure at all). Both PDFs read directly; DOIs in channel-d.
2. **The null with stated search paths** (§1 P4). Say plainly: nobody can show any of it predicts
   anything — no backtest, no calibration curve, from vendor, analyst, or academic.
3. **The tautology.** Coverage = 1/win-rate; "3x" is a ~33% win rate restated. Three unrelated
   packs + a live practitioner tweet carry it uncited. Teach the derivation, retire the constant.
4. **The small-numbers gate.** Calibration is a property of a track record (Brier n>1000 for rare
   events), not of one deal's "60%". A solo pipeline can never validate stage weights — which
   frees the reader from pretending.
5. **The constructive primitives** (what to do instead, all runnable at n=1):
   - **patio11's prediction-then-score loop** — write "if we lose this deal it will be because of
     X" IN ADVANCE; check after. Serves forecasting honesty AND win/loss. The only calibration
     mechanism found on any surface. (x.com/patio11/status/1345906043197427713)
   - **KyleAsay's NEGATIVE exit criteria** — no velocity / no concerns ("no concerns often means
     no consideration") / no next-best-option — reasons to REFUSE a forecast, the inversion of
     hand-set stage weights.
   - **Sourcegraph's floor/ceiling/judged-middle** — Commit is summed, Call is judged; the
     headline number is openly a judgment (Apache-2.0, archived-2024 caveat must travel).
   - **GitLab's estimated-until-quoted boundary** — a deal's value is an estimate until a quote
     exists; every pipeline total mixes estimated and quoted amounts without disclosing the ratio.
   - **thaAdamLittle as the metric-hygiene exemplar** — names, defines, and ranks his own metric,
     labels his rule "Rough heuristic" (the positive model against "3x coverage").

**Strong second (flagship-grade file, not the flagship): win/loss validity.** Nisbett & Wilson
1977 — stated reasons are post-hoc plausible theories; observers reproduce the same "reasons"
without having been in the room; a stated reason carries no privileged causal information. Carry
the boundary condition honestly (accurate reports occur when influential stimuli are salient and
plausible). Nobody in the incumbent space asks the validity question (one vendor page spot-checked
— scope that claim narrowly). The patio11 primitive is the answer here too — one through-line,
two jobs.

Unlike success (all six failed), sales strips split three ways — teach the categories:

   different quantity, same lead researcher, and the live Gartner URL no longer contains it).

   not circulate as peer conversation on X (b4, one-phrasing caveat) — teach
   recognition-in-published-material, not myth-busting. And teach the SHAPE as a genre (A): the
   decision-already-made figure appears as 57%, webdesign's "9 in 10", Shipley's "70–80%" — three
   numbers, one rhetorical job, no traceable source among the derivatives.
2. **Pure folklore, no defensible version:** "92% give up after 4 nos", "80% need 5 follow-ups"
   (anonymous dead blog naming no study; internal contradictions between versions), BANT's IBM
   origin, "always be closing" as pedigree (Blake is a 1992 film invention, absent from Mamet's
   play — a satirical antagonist adopted as doctrine).
3. **Vendor-research genre — real N, undisclosed method:** Gong Labs (own-customer sample,
